GNT Landscaping runs out of Cranberry Township, Butler County, and most of our crews are on a customer's property inside thirty minutes of leaving the yard on Kingston Avenue. That radius reaches across three counties — Butler, Allegheny and Beaver — and covers roughly forty townships, boroughs and communities through the northern Pittsburgh suburbs.

Being genuinely local to this stretch of Western Pennsylvania matters more than it sounds. The ground here is difficult in specific, predictable ways: heavy clay that holds water instead of shedding it, close to forty inches of rain a year, a freeze-thaw cycle that lifts anything set on a shallow base, and hillsides steep enough that a patio needs a retaining wall before it needs pavers. A contractor who works this soil every week builds differently from one who drives up from a flatter market for the day.

Butler County, PA — our home county

Butler County is where the business is registered, where the yard is, and where the majority of our work happens. Crews are in the county six days a week from spring through the end of leaf season, then back through the winter on snow and ice contracts.

Cranberry Township

Cranberry Township is our home base and our busiest single market. The township grew quickly along the I‑79 and PA‑228 corridor, and that growth left a particular signature on its yards: newer subdivisions where construction traffic compacted the subgrade hard, then a thin skim of topsoil was spread over the top and seeded. The result is lawns that struggle in July and yards that hold standing water for days after a storm.

That is why so much of our Cranberry Township work starts underground rather than with plants. French drain installation, regrading, downspout extensions and dry creek beds come before new sod, because sod laid over compacted clay simply fails a second time. Once water is moving where it should, we build the visible part: paver patios, retaining walls, planting beds, mulch installation and landscape lighting.

Jackson Township, Zelienople and Harmony

Jackson Township wraps around the boroughs of Zelienople and Harmony, immediately west of Cranberry. Lots here run larger than the Cranberry subdivisions and the housing stock is more mixed — older homes on established ground alongside newer builds. Older properties often have mature trees worth designing around, along with drainage that was adequate for a smaller roof and a shorter driveway and is no longer keeping up.

Zelienople and Harmony themselves have tighter, older lots where access is the constraint. We size equipment to the property rather than the reverse, which sometimes means hauling base material by hand through a side yard to avoid tearing up a lawn we would then have to repair.

Adams Township, Mars and Seven Fields

Adams Township, Mars borough and Seven Fields sit just east and north of Cranberry and share its growth pattern. Demand here leans toward outdoor living: paver patios, fire pits, seating walls, pergolas and low-voltage landscape lighting that makes the space usable after dinner. Backyard renovations in these neighbourhoods are frequently a matter of replacing a builder-grade concrete slab that has cracked along its control joints with a properly based paver installation.

Butler, Butler Township and the northern townships

North of the growth corridor the county opens up. Butler city, Butler Township, Penn Township, Middlesex Township, Connoquenessing Township, Clinton Township, Buffalo Township and Forward Township bring larger properties, longer driveways and more rural conditions — which changes the work rather than reducing it. Bigger lots mean hydroseeding rather than sod on the large open areas, gravel and stone driveways that need periodic regrading, and drainage problems that arrive from neighbouring ground rather than from the house itself.

Saxonburg, Valencia and Callery round out the boroughs we cover on this side of the county.

Allegheny County, PA and the North Hills

South of Cranberry the terrain changes character. Allegheny County's North Hills is genuinely hilly, and slope drives nearly every decision on a property — where a patio can physically sit, how many tiers a retaining wall needs, and where the water coming off the hill above is going to end up.

Pine Township, Marshall Township and Wexford

Pine and Marshall townships, along with the Wexford community inside them, are among our steadier Allegheny County markets. Properties here tend to be larger and more heavily wooded, which brings its own set of problems: dense shade where turf will not establish, root competition, and leaf load heavy enough to smother a lawn if it sits through winter. Shade-tolerant seed blends, selective canopy work and, on the sites where grass genuinely will not grow, artificial turf installation all get used here.

McCandless, Hampton, Richland and Gibsonia

McCandless Township, Hampton Township, Richland Township and the Gibsonia community make up the eastern side of our Allegheny County coverage. This is established, mature suburbia — homes several decades old with landscaping installed when they were built and long since outgrown. A great deal of our work here is renovation rather than installation: taking out overgrown foundation shrubs planted too close to the house, rebuilding failed timber retaining walls in segmental block, and re-cutting bed lines that have crept over the years.

Sewickley, Fox Chapel, Franklin Park and Bradford Woods

Sewickley, Fox Chapel, Franklin Park, Bradford Woods, Ohio Township and Indiana Township are our detail-driven markets. Expectations on finish are high, properties are often visible from the street on all sides, and deer pressure is heavy enough that plant selection has to be deliberate — a beautifully designed bed of hostas and arborvitae is a feeding station in these neighbourhoods. We plan around it with deer-resistant species and, where a client wants something the deer will otherwise take, physical protection through the establishment years.

Pittsburgh

We take work in the City of Pittsburgh itself, primarily in the northern neighbourhoods and primarily on larger projects where the travel is justified. City lots bring access limits, permit considerations and neighbours close enough that site cleanliness each evening is not optional.

Beaver County, PA

Beaver County is our newest area of coverage and it sits naturally at the western edge of the existing radius.

Daugherty Township and Marion Township

Daugherty Township, near New Brighton, and Marion Township both bring a mix of residential lots and more open, rural ground. The larger open areas here suit hydroseeding, which covers more square footage per dollar than sod and establishes well when the timing and the watering plan are right. Slope and river-valley drainage are the recurring engineering problems on this side of the service area.

New Sewickley and North Sewickley Township

New Sewickley Township and North Sewickley Township bridge the gap between Beaver County and the Zelienople side of Butler County, which makes them straightforward for us to schedule alongside existing routes. New Brighton, Beaver and Economy boroughs complete our Beaver County coverage.

What the ground in Western PA actually demands

Four conditions shape nearly every project we build, and they apply from Cranberry Township to Beaver County.

Clay soil that will not drain on its own

Western Pennsylvania clay holds water. On a flat or gently sloped lot that means saturated ground for days after heavy rain, and water finding its way toward the lowest point — which is very often a basement wall. French drains, dry creek beds, downspout extensions and honest regrading are the tools that fix it. Yard drainage is not a glamorous line on a proposal, but on a lot with a water problem it is the only work that makes everything above it last.

A freeze-thaw cycle that lifts bad workmanship

Ground that freezes and thaws repeatedly through the winter moves, and it takes shallow construction with it. Patios settle, walkways heave into trip hazards, and wall blocks push out of course. The defence is base depth and drainage: a properly compacted aggregate base, geotextile separation where the subgrade calls for it, footings below the local frost line, and drainage behind every retaining wall. It is the part of a hardscape nobody sees and the reason one patio still looks right after ten winters while its neighbour does not.

Lawns built on construction subgrade

Any lawn on a house built in the last twenty-five years is likely sitting on ground that was driven over by heavy equipment for months. Compacted subgrade under two inches of topsoil cannot support turf through a dry August no matter how much water it gets. Real sod installation and new lawn work therefore starts with decompaction, grading and soil amendment — then sod, seed or hydroseed, with a watering schedule that actually gets it rooted.

Snow and ice, roughly forty inches of it

An average winter here brings forty-odd inches of snow, and more in the northern reaches of Butler County. Our commercial snow and ice management contracts cover plowing, salting and sidewalk clearing for offices, retail sites, HOAs and stratas across the service area, with 24-hour call-out through the season. Contracts are set before winter starts, because the properties that wait until the first storm are the ones that cannot find a contractor.

If your town is not on the list

The list above is where we work routinely and can schedule efficiently. It is not a fence. For larger builds — a full backyard renovation, a substantial retaining wall, a complete landscape installation — we travel further, including into Lawrence County and the wider Pittsburgh area. What changes on a longer haul is scheduling and mobilisation, not the standard of the work.

The honest answer for a smaller job well outside the radius is usually that a closer contractor will serve you better, and we will say so rather than take the work and give you a worse result. Call and describe the property and we will tell you straight away whether it makes sense.

Getting started

Every project begins the same way regardless of which county the property sits in. We walk the site, take measurements, and look at where the water goes before we look at anything else. You get a fixed-price proposal with every line itemised, so you can see exactly what the base preparation costs versus the visible finishes. There is no charge for the estimate and no obligation attached to it.

Once work starts, one crew and one schedule see it through, the site is cleaned every evening, and you finish with a written warranty and a care guide for what has been planted or installed.
